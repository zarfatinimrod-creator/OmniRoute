# Research findings (raw, pre-synthesis) - 2026-11-09



######## llm-trading-agent-repos: open-source LLM and multi-agent trading systems (2024-2026), and what a self-improving, Claude-driven paper trader should borrow
Star counts and dates are from the GitHub search API on 2026-11-09. I read the code from shallow clones of 13 of the repos; several others I judged from their README only. GitHub's repo-scoped API is blocked from this machine, but search, raw.githubusercontent and `git clone` all work. TauricResearch/TradingAgents (121,456 stars, Apache-2.0, v0.7.1, last push 2026-11-04) is the reference design: four analysts work in parallel, then a bull/bear debate, a research manager, a trader, an aggressive/conservative/neutral risk debate, and a portfolio manager who decides on a 5-tier rating. Its learning loop is simple and worth copying. Every decision goes into an append-only markdown log. After 5 trading days the decision is scored on raw return and alpha vs SPY, an LLM writes a 2-4 sentence lesson, and the portfolio manager later reads the last 5 decisions on the same ticker plus 3 lessons from other tickers, filtered by date so backtests cannot see the future. Its published results do not hold up: a 3-month backtest (Jan to Mar 2024) on 3 stocks, a Sharpe of 8.21 that the authors themselves call above the expected range, and about 11 LLM calls plus 20+ tool calls per decision. virattt/ai-hedge-fund (64.6k stars, MIT) is now 'aihf' v2.7.1. Its design is the best of the group: the LLM never places orders, decisions made at T execute at T+1, the ledger is append-only and hash-chained, there is a kill switch, every analyst is graded on hit rate, slugging and IC with a t-stat, and risk limits only ever shrink positions. But the package pins numpy<2 and pandas<3, so it will not install cleanly on our Python 3.13 / pandas 3 container, and it needs the paid Financial Datasets API. The closest match to our project is a small repo, chase-manning/ai-hedge-fund (97 stars, MIT, Python 3.13, 8 dependencies): every 4 hours Claude proposes a target weight, a 24h probability, a stop and a thesis per market; deterministic code gates and executes; paper 'shadow' baselines run alongside; scoring includes Brier score and calibration; and it only evaluates forward in time. HKUDS/Vibe-Trading (37.9k, MIT, very active) and tradermonty/claude-trading-skills (3.7k, MIT) are the richest sources of SKILL.md files to borrow (89 and 74 skills), including journaling, thesis tracking, a postmortem, a drawdown circuit breaker and a pre-trade discipline gate. Vibe-Trading as a whole package is too heavy to install. The academic repos are mostly dead or empty: FinMem was last pushed 2024-08, FinCon never released its code, FinAgent is abandoned, and FinGPT is 2023 Llama-2 LoRA models. HKUDS/AI-Trader has turned into a social copy-trading site with no LICENSE file. TradingAgents-CN is aimed at Chinese A-shares, and its app/ and frontend/ directories are under a proprietary license. Independent benchmarks agree: FINSABER (two decades, 100+ symbols), StockBench, AI-Trader and LiveTradeBench all find that most LLM agents fail to beat buy-and-hold or manage risk poorly, and that published LLM edges shrink under longer, broader tests. One live real-money run, ChatGPT managing $100 in micro-caps, hit a 50% max drawdown. So we should vendor no framework as the core. Build a thin pipeline where Claude proposes and code decides, borrow the specific patterns above, copy selected MIT skills, and judge the bot only on graded paper decisions made going forward, never on LLM backtests of dates the model may already have seen in training.

RECS:
- Vendor no framework as the core. Build our own thin pipeline (Python 3.13, anthropic SDK, pandas 3, yfinance) on the 'Claude proposes, code disposes' pattern from chase-manning/ai-hedge-fund and aihf v2. Claude outputs structured views; deterministic code owns sizing, limits, orders and keys. Execute next session (decide at T, fill at T+1 open or close) in both paper and backtest modes.
- Decision schema per ticker (pydantic, enforced through tool or JSON output): rating in {Buy, Overweight, Hold, Underweight, Sell} with REVIEW as the fallback when output cannot be parsed, never a silent Hold (from TradingAgents rating.py); p_outperform_SPY over horizon_days (a calibrated probability, from chase-manning); target_weight; stop_pct; entry thesis in 5 sentences or fewer; 2-4 falsifiable invalidation conditions and red lines (from Vibe-Trading thesis-tracker); horizon_days; data snapshot hash.
- Journal: append-only JSONL, each record SHA-256-chained to the previous one (aihf ledger / tradememory). Use (date, ticker) as the idempotency key, log HOLD and no-trade decisions too, and never edit a prediction after logging (an amendment is a new linked record, per thesis-investment-os). Replay verifies the chain at startup and halts on a mismatch (fail loud).
- Settlement and learning job, run every session: once a record's horizon has traded, compute raw return and alpha vs SPY from the first close after the decision, plus Brier/log-loss and hit/slugging. Have Claude write a 2-4 sentence reflection using TradingAgents' Reflector prompt (port memory/reflection.py, Apache-2.0, with notice). Before each new decision, inject the last 5 same-ticker records, 3 cross-ticker lessons and the top losing trades in similar conditions (the tradememory losses-first idea), ranked with FinMem's score: similarity + exp(-Δdays/Q) + importance/100, with Q=3/90/365 by layer.
- Self-improvement must be statistically gated. Keep an aihf-style scorecard per signal source (technical, news, fundamentals, each debate role): hit rate, slugging, daily cross-sectional IC with t-stat. Re-weight the ensemble or retire a source only after 30 or more graded views and |t| > 2. Let Claude revise a small versioned beliefs.md after risk events (FinCon idea), but log every rule change and show it to the user. Never auto-tune on fewer than about 100 graded decisions.
- Always run shadow baselines on paper next to the bot (SPY buy-and-hold, 20-day trend rule, equal-weight watchlist; chase-manning baselines.py) and report the bot's alpha against them. The benchmarks above show most LLM agents do not beat buy-and-hold, so the default hypothesis is 'no edge' until the forward record says otherwise.
- Deterministic risk gate. Start with: per-name 20% or less (lower for small accounts), gross 100% or less (no leverage), 1% of equity risked per trade at the stop, 25% max single-symbol exposure, stop on every entry, flip cooldown about 12h, no re-entry for N days after a stop-out, halt new risk at 3-4% daily loss or 10-20% drawdown, capped exposure ahead of earnings and binary events, and no micro-caps. These come from the AlpacaTradingAgent and chase-manning defaults plus the LLM-Trading-Lab failure modes. Clamps only shrink and removed exposure stays in cash (aihf risk/limits.py).
- Spend tokens like TradingAgents does, but only where it helps. Use cheap screening and indicators in code. Run the full analyst → bull/bear (1 round) → risk debate → PM committee only on the few candidates that pass deterministic filters. Use a quick Claude model for analysts and debaters and Opus for the PM, matching TradingAgents' quick/deep tiers. Budget per cycle; TradingAgents needs about 12 or more LLM calls per ticker per date.
- Evaluate going forward only. Any LLM backtest over dates before the model's training cutoff (June 2026 for Opus 5.5) is contaminated by memorization (Gao/Jiang/Yan lookahead-bias test, arXiv 2512.23847; chase-manning README). Use code-only backtests for the deterministic parts, and paper-trade forward for the LLM parts.
- Install these skills (copy the MIT folders with the LICENSE into the project's .claude/skills). From tradermonty/claude-trading-skills: trader-memory-core, signal-postmortem, drawdown-circuit-breaker, pre-trade-discipline-gate, position-sizer, exposure-coach, market-breadth-analyzer, macro-regime-detector, data-quality-checker, backtest-expert, weekly-performance-digest. From HKUDS/Vibe-Trading agent/src/skills: research-discipline, thesis-tracker, trade-journal, backtest-diagnose, risk-analysis, quant-statistics, earnings-revision, correlation-regime.
- Optional extras, each in its own venv and never on the critical path: TradingAgents pinned to commit 9fa1196, as an on-demand deep-dive report with deep_think_provider='anthropic'; tradememory-protocol as an MCP memory and order brake in front of alpacahq/alpaca-mcp-server once paper keys exist. Network notes for that setup: set SEC_EDGAR_USER_AGENT, disable FRED and Reddit vendors (FRED timed out and Reddit returned 403 from this machine), and keep Yahoo as the primary free data source (HTTP 200 here).
- Security: never let the bot fetch and follow remote instruction files (e.g. the 'Read https://ai4trade.ai/SKILL.md and register' pattern in HKUDS/AI-Trader). Pin vendored code to commits, review copied SKILL.md files before use, and keep broker keys out of LLM context.

######## liquid-trade-and-alt-data: what "Liquid trade" is, and which alternative-data sources to use alongside Quiver
"Liquid trade" is almost certainly Liquid (liquid.trade, run by LiquidX AI, Inc.). It is a non-custodial, Paradigm-backed app that raised an $18M seed in April 2026. It trades 24/7 perpetual futures (perps) on US stocks, indices, FX, commodities, crypto and pre-IPO names, and its own ToS describes the app as a front end on Hyperliquid smart contracts. It fits the bot well: it has a built-in paper-trading mode and a hosted MCP server called Co-Invest. The variant for autonomous agents, "Co-Invest Computer" (https://coinvest-computer.liquid.trade/mcp), has a Paper mode in which an agent trades a simulated balance. Auth is OAuth 2.1 with PKCE, dynamic client registration and refresh tokens (I checked the live metadata), so a headless Python bot can run after one browser approval. The big caveat: Liquid "stocks" are leveraged perps (up to 50x, funding payments, liquidation risk), not shares. So Liquid suits signal and paper execution, while Alpaca paper is the realistic equities broker. Hyperliquid itself is the second-best reading. Its free, no-auth info API serves 144 equity/commodity perps on the "xyz" dex (live query: NVDA about $272M in 24h volume). Its testnet has 70 xyz markets, but the faucet needs a prior mainnet deposit from the same address. Other "Liquid"s are irrelevant: Liquid.com/Quoine (FTX subsidiary, halted Nov 2022), Liquidity Group (Israeli private-credit fintech), LiquidTrade (institutional order-management system), Liquid AI (LLM vendor). For alternative data, the strongest free stack is: SEC EDGAR Form 4 (free, 10 req/s, needs a contact User-Agent; edgartools is MIT with 2.8k stars), OpenInsider HTML (scrape confirmed working), and Finnhub's free tier. Finnhub's free endpoints cover insider transactions, insider sentiment, lobbying, government spending, company news, recommendation trends and earnings calendar; congressional trading, news sentiment and social sentiment are Premium. Massive (formerly Polygon) Basic is free at 5 calls/min and includes news with per-ticker sentiment. Alpha Vantage free (25 calls/day) offers NEWS_SENTIMENT and INSIDER_TRANSACTIONS. Best-value paid options: FMP Starter at $29/mo ($19/mo billed annually) for Senate and House trades plus insider data, as a Quiver alternative or cross-check. Quiver Hobbyist is $30/mo; Quiver Trader at $75/mo adds insider and 13F data. Unusual Whales ($99 to $150/mo) is the only option worth paying for options flow and dark-pool data, and only after the bot shows an edge. On the evidence: opportunistic insider buys carry the best-documented edge, 82 bps/month abnormal return (Cohen, Malloy and Pomorski 2012). Rank-and-file congressional trades show no outperformance after the STOCK Act (Belmont et al. 2022). Congressional leaders are a possible exception, about 47 percentage points a year, but that rests on a small sample and an unreviewed NBER working paper. So weight insider data above congressional data in the ensemble.

######## quiver-quant-api
Quiver Quantitative's REST API lives at https://api.quiverquant.com. All documented endpoints sit under /beta/ and return JSON. ######## continuous-ops-architecture: near-zero-cost continuous US-market monitoring, alerts, state and failure handling for a paper-money swing-trading bot run by an Israeli retail user
Recommended split: a cheap, deterministic "body" and a slow, smart "brain". The body is Python on GitHub Actions cron in a PRIVATE repo, with about 4 fixed slots per trading day timed in America/New_York. It fetches data, scores signals, writes the journal and sends Telegram alerts. The brain is one Claude Code cloud scheduled task per trading day (plus one weekly) that grades predictions, writes lessons and proposes parameter changes as a PR. It can also be fired on demand through its /fire API when the body detects an anomaly. Claude cloud tasks are a poor fit for the polling loop. They reject anything more frequent than hourly, every run spends subscription usage, and (per this harness's tool schema, not public docs) runs starting 9am-noon ET can be delayed, which is exactly the US open. GitHub Actions cron is best-effort. GitHub's own docs say scheduled runs can be delayed at high load (especially at the top of the hour) and queued jobs can be DROPPED. Schedules run only from the default branch, are disabled in forks of public repos, and in public repos are auto-disabled after 60 days without activity. The minimum interval is 5 minutes, and there is now a per-cron `timezone:` key. So the design must be idempotent: a run-key ledger, alert de-duplication, catch-up of missed slots, a single-writer concurrency group, and pull --rebase retry pushes. An external dead-man's switch (healthchecks.io free) is also needed, because a dropped run does not fail, it just never happens. Cost is effectively zero. Public repos get free standard runners. A private repo on GitHub Free gets $12/month of compute ($0.006/min on 2-core Linux, $0.002/min on 1-core ubuntu-slim, each job rounded up to a whole minute), and about 85 runs/month uses roughly $0.5-1. For a swing bot, intraday polling adds little alpha. Its real value is operational: a post-open gap check, a pre-close slot that can submit Alpaca market-on-close/limit-on-close orders (TIF `cls`, cutoff 15:50 ET), and an end-of-day final-bar run. Intraday risk is better handled by broker-side bracket and trailing-stop orders than by a VPS. A dedicated always-on machine is only justified for IBKR Gateway (a continuous process with weekly re-authentication), websocket streaming, or sub-minute reactions. State should be append-only JSONL committed to a dedicated `bot-state` branch, not SQLite blobs in git, Gist or Supabase. I verified the NYSE calendar against nyse.com: exchange_calendars 4.13.2 and pandas_market_calendars 5.6.0 both match the official 2026-2027 holidays and the three 1pm early closes. One gotcha: exchange_calendars' default calendar ends exactly one year from today. ######## ml-llm-prediction-reality: what ML/LLM stock prediction can realistically do in 2026, and how to evaluate it honestly
Honest stock prediction works with thin edges. Even the best published signals get tradable directional hit rates of about 51-58%. A naive "always up" call on SPY was right on 55% of days, 60% of weeks and 68% of months from 2005 to 2026 (computed from Yahoo data), so a raw 55% accuracy figure proves nothing until it is compared with that base rate.

1. **Boosted trees on engineered features.** Gu-Kelly-Xiu is the main reference. Trees and neural nets reached a monthly out-of-sample stock-level R² of only 0.33-0.40% (OLS on all predictors: -3.46%). That still gave value-weighted long-short Sharpe ratios around 1.35. Microsoft Qlib's public benchmark tells the same story: LightGBM/XGBoost reach IC about 0.045-0.050, a plain linear model 0.040, and LSTM/Transformer models do worse. Later work, however, shows much of this academic ML alpha sits in microcaps and distressed stocks and fades after trading costs and publication.

2. **LLM news sentiment.** Lopez-Lira & Tang is real but often misread. GPT-4's roughly 90% hit rate is for the initial price reaction, which you cannot trade. The tradable next-day drift hit rate is 55-58%. The strategy's Sharpe fell from 6.54 (2021Q4) to 1.22 (Jan-May 2024) as LLM adoption spread, and 20 bps round-trip cost wipes it out.

3. **Time-series foundation models (Chronos, TimesFM, Kronos).** Off-the-shelf they mostly do not beat naive baselines on equity returns:
   - Zero-shot R² was negative (TimesFM directional accuracy just below 50%).
   - A June 2026 benchmark found gains over a random walk "small and sparse", significant in only 2 cases.
   - Kronos claims +93% RankIC, but that is relative to very small absolute ICs (about 0.02-0.07), tested mostly on Chinese A-shares.

4. **Look-ahead and memorization in LLM backtests.** This is the biggest trap. LLMs recall exact pre-cutoff prices and macro numbers; telling them to ignore what they know fails, and so does masking names. LLM trading agents lose 51-62% of their Sharpe once tested after the model's cutoff (Profit Mirage). FINSABER (KDD 2026) shows earlier LLM-agent claims came from 3-6-month windows on 3-8 stocks and disappear over 20 years and 100+ symbols. LLMs were too cautious in bull markets and lost heavily in bear markets.

5. **Live tests.** StockBench, LiveTradeBench and nof1's Alpha Arena all confirm it. Most LLMs fail to beat buy-and-hold. Alpha Arena Season 1 had four of six frontier models losing 42-59% in two weeks. nof1 itself now says current LLMs are "shockingly bad at navigating financial markets".

6. **Where LLMs do help.** The credible positive results come from LLMs used as interpreters or overlays on top of an established factor, scored only after the model's cutoff or collected live as forecasts are made:
   - Momentum plus an LLM news filter: out-of-sample Sharpe 0.79 to 1.06.
   - Daily agentic forecasts collected live: alpha only in the top-20 picks, over a short 2025 sample.

**What this means for the bot:**
- Use boosted trees as the core ranker.
- Use the LLM to interpret events, veto risky trades, explain decisions and tag post-mortems, never as a price oracle.
- Judge the LLM only on predictions journaled live, after its cutoff, and measure calibration with Brier scores against base-rate benchmarks.

RECS:
- Use a gradient-boosted cross-sectional ranker (LightGBM, 5-21 day forward excess return, liquid US large and mid caps) as the bot's predictive core. Aim for a realistic rank IC of 0.02-0.05 and a weekly hit rate a few points above the base rate, not 60%+ accuracy.
- Never give the LLM raw price prediction or position sizing. Use it for: (a) event interpretation (earnings releases, 8-K, guidance and analyst changes turned into structured JSON features); (b) a risk veto (dilution, litigation, fraud, going-concern, binary FDA or merger events); (c) a filter or overlay on candidates from the quant ranker (the Anic et al. momentum pattern); (d) Hebrew explanations for each alert; (e) post-mortem tagging of journaled mistakes.
- Enforce a hard post-cutoff rule in code: LLM-derived signals are scored only on predictions timestamped live, after the model's knowledge cutoff. Store model_id, prompt hash, inputs and timestamp before the market moves. Never cite pre-cutoff LLM backtests as evidence.
- Ship a mandatory baseline panel in every report: SPY buy-and-hold, always-up classifier (55% of days, 60% of weeks, 68% of months on SPY 2005-2026), equal-weight universe, 12-1 momentum, and random entry with matched turnover. A signal that cannot beat these net of 10-20 bps round-trip costs gets no capital, paper or real.
- Size expectations statistically. About 385 independent bets are needed to distinguish a 55% hit rate from 50%, about 2,400 for 52%. Confirming Sharpe 1.0 at t=2 takes about 4 years of live data. Treat 3-6 months of paper trading as a sanity check (execution, bugs, calibration), not proof of edge.
- Make the 'learning from mistakes' loop resistant to overfitting. Log every variant tried (n_trials), promote changes only via purged walk-forward CV plus Deflated Sharpe > 0.95, retrain monthly, and auto-disable a signal when its rolling 60-day rank IC goes negative or its Brier skill score falls below 0.
- Have the LLM emit explicit probabilities for resolvable events and horizon direction. Score them monthly with Brier score and reliability curves, recalibrate with isotonic regression once there are 200+ outcomes, and shrink toward base rates, because RLHF models are verbally overconfident.
- Put FINSABER's lesson into the risk layer: regime detection (e.g., SPY below its 200-day moving average or a volatility spike → cut gross exposure) and code-enforced stops. LLM agents are documented to fail by being too timid in bull markets and too aggressive in bear markets.
- Treat Kronos, TimesFM and Chronos as optional features for volatility or volume forecasts (position sizing and stops).
